## GLM-4.5 (2025)

### 1. Overview & Strategic Context

GLM-4.5 (Zhipu AI, released mid-2025) is a sharp architectural break from GLM-4: where GLM-4 was a dense model in the lineage's original General-Language-Model-objective tradition (see `010_GLM4.md`), GLM-4.5 is a **sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE) model**, reportedly **~355B total parameters with ~32B active parameters per token** via top-k expert routing. This figure is the best publicly available number from Zhipu's own release materials — it is **not** independently re-verified in this document, and should be read with the same reported-not-audited caveat applied to any lab's own parameter-count disclosure elsewhere in this folder. Zhipu also released a smaller sibling, **GLM-4.5-Air**, at a reduced total/active parameter budget, giving the family a two-point size ladder rather than Qwen2.5's full ladder or DeepSeek's single-flagship-plus-distillations approach.

The headline capability story is **hybrid reasoning**: GLM-4.5 exposes a thinking-mode toggle that lets the same checkpoint run either as a fast, direct-response model or as an extended-reasoning model that emits deliberation before its final answer. This is the same *design category* as Claude 3.7 Sonnet's extended-thinking toggle (`Claude/005_Claude3_7_Extended_Thinking.md` in this collection) — one set of weights, a request-time mode switch, no separate checkpoint for "the reasoning model." It is a meaningfully different bet from OpenAI's GPT-5 framing (`GPT/009_GPT4_1_And_GPT5.md`), where the disclosed design is a *system* of at least two distinct models (a fast tier and a deeper-reasoning tier) plus a router that decides which one handles a given query — GPT-5's hybrid-ness lives in the routing layer *between* models, while GLM-4.5's (like Claude 3.7's) lives *inside* one model as a trainable behavior mode. This is a clean, concrete illustration of the same underlying industry trend (test-time compute as a controllable resource) being solved via two structurally different serving architectures, and it is worth having ready as an interview connection.

Zhipu's second strategic emphasis for this generation is **agentic tool-use and coding capability**, with GLM-4.5 explicitly positioned and benchmarked around function-calling, tool-use, and agentic coding tasks rather than static knowledge benchmarks alone. Zhipu reports GLM-4.5 as competitive with, and on some specific agentic/coding benchmarks exceeding, some Western frontier models at time of release — this is a self-reported, single-lab comparison and should be treated with the calibrated skepticism this document set applies to every lab's own cross-competitor claims (see Qwen2.5's and GLM-4's equivalent sections). What is not in dispute is the release mechanics: GLM-4.5 was released **open-weight under a permissive (MIT) license**, making it one of the strongest fully open-weight MoE reasoning models to come out of a Chinese lab, alongside DeepSeek (`006`–`008` in this folder) and Qwen (`009`). Around this generation, Zhipu's public/product-facing brand shifted to **"Z.ai"** as the international-facing surface for the company's models and API, while "Zhipu AI" remains the underlying research/engineering organization — a branding detail worth knowing but not a claim about any architectural change.

### 2. Architecture Deep Dive

**MoE routing design.** GLM-4.5 follows the now-familiar fine-grained MoE pattern also used by DeepSeek-V2/V3 (`006`, `007`) and, in coarser form, Mixtral (`005`): a bank of routed experts plus one or more always-on shared experts, with a learned router producing per-token affinity scores, a top-k selection of routed experts, and a weighted combination of the selected experts' outputs. At GLM-4.5's reported ~355B-total/~32B-active scale, active parameters are roughly **9%** of total — a sparsity ratio in the same neighborhood as DeepSeek-V3's ~5.5% (37B/671B) and considerably sparser than Mixtral's ~28% (12.9B/46.7B), consistent with the general 2024–2025 trend toward larger total-parameter pools with a roughly fixed or even shrinking active-parameter compute budget per token. The precise load-balancing mechanism Zhipu uses (an explicit auxiliary load-balancing loss in the Switch/Mixtral tradition, versus DeepSeek-V3's auxiliary-loss-free bias-based feedback scheme) is **less consistently detailed in public secondary sources than DeepSeek's own dedicated disclosure** — this document describes the general top-k-with-shared-experts mechanism with confidence and flags the specific balancing algorithm as a genuinely less-certain point (see Section 11) rather than asserting it matches either prior scheme.

**Hybrid-reasoning toggle mechanism.** As with Claude 3.7 Sonnet, there is no disclosed evidence of a separate "reasoning module" bolted onto a "chat module" — hybrid reasoning is exposed as a mode on the same model endpoint, strongly implying it is a trained behavior (most plausibly instilled via SFT on reasoning traces followed by outcome-based RL, the same general recipe DeepSeek-R1 (`008`) and other 2024–2025 reasoning models use) rather than an architectural bolt-on. Mechanically, when thinking mode is enabled, the model generates an extended deliberation span (structurally comparable to DeepSeek-R1's `<think>...</think>` tagging and to Claude's `thinking` content blocks) before producing its final response; when thinking mode is disabled, the same weights produce a direct response without that intermediate span. The key architectural point worth stating precisely in an interview: **this is a single-checkpoint mode switch, not a router selecting between two different models** — the distinction that separates GLM-4.5's and Claude's hybrid-reasoning framing from GPT-5's system-of-models-plus-router framing (Section 1). Whether GLM-4.5 exposes a continuous "thinking budget" (Claude's `budget_tokens`-style dial) or a coarser binary/tiered toggle is a serving-API detail that is less uniformly documented across secondary sources than the underlying two-mode capability itself.

### 3. Scale — Parameters, Data, Compute

- **GLM-4.5**: reportedly ~355B total parameters, ~32B active parameters per token (top-k MoE routing). **Best-available-public figure, not independently re-verified here.**
- **GLM-4.5-Air**: a smaller sibling at a reduced total/active parameter budget, released alongside the flagship as a lighter-weight deployment option — the exact figures are Zhipu's own disclosure and, like the flagship's, are treated as reported rather than audited.
- Context window: Zhipu reports a context length in the same 100K+ tier common to 2025-era frontier open-weight releases; exact figure and whether it required post-hoc RoPE-style extension (as with DeepSeek-V2's and Qwen2.5's YaRN-based extensions) versus native long-context pretraining is not detailed here with the same confidence as the parameter counts.
- Training data composition, size, and mixture (English/Chinese ratio, code/math upsampling, synthetic-data usage) are **not disclosed at anything like DeepSeek-V3's or even Qwen2.5's level of specificity** — this is the single largest disclosure gap for GLM-4.5 relative to its most directly comparable open-weight MoE peers in this folder, and is flagged explicitly rather than filled in with an inferred number.
- License: released under a permissive MIT license, more permissive than Llama's community license and comparable in spirit to Mistral's Apache-2.0-licensed releases.

### 4. Training Infrastructure & Distributed Training

At a reported ~355B total / ~32B active parameter scale, the routed-expert pool cannot fit in a single accelerator's or even a single node's memory, so **expert parallelism** — sharding the routed-expert bank across devices/nodes and dispatching each token's activations to whichever device holds its selected top-k experts, then combining outputs back — is a structural necessity, combined with the standard data- and pipeline-parallelism layers used throughout this document set's MoE models. The core systems cost at this scale is the all-to-all communication volume incurred by token dispatch/combine across expert-parallel ranks, the same bottleneck DeepSeek-V2's device-limited routing (`006`) and DeepSeek-V3's communication-aware engineering (`007`) are explicitly built to manage. Zhipu's public disclosure of GLM-4.5's specific cluster configuration, interconnect, expert-parallelism degree, and training wall-clock/cost is considerably thinner than DeepSeek-V3's technical report — treat any specific infrastructure number for GLM-4.5 beyond the parameter-count figures above as undisclosed rather than inferable.

### 5. Pretraining Data & Objective

Standard autoregressive next-token prediction is the presumed base pretraining objective — Zhipu has not disclosed a departure from this for GLM-4.5's base pretraining, and the earlier GLM-lineage blank-infilling objective (`010_GLM4.md`, Section 2) does not appear to be the headline objective at this generation (consistent with the Section 2 nuance already flagged in that file, that the practical GLM recipe had converged toward conventional causal-LM-style pretraining by the GLM-4 generation). Beyond that presumption, exact training corpus size, composition, and any synthetic-data or agentic-trajectory-generation component used to support the model's tool-use/coding emphasis are not disclosed with the specificity this document set applies to DeepSeek-V3 or Qwen2.5 — this is one of the genuinely open questions for this model (Section 11).

### 6. Post-Training / Alignment Approach

GLM-4.5's post-training must accomplish two distinguishable things: (1) instill the hybrid-reasoning behavior itself — most plausibly via SFT on reasoning traces followed by outcome-based RL rewarding correct final answers reached through the thinking process, the same general playbook described for DeepSeek-R1's RLVR/GRPO pipeline (`008`) and for Claude 3.7's presumed (Anthropic has not disclosed specifics) RL-based approach; and (2) instill strong agentic tool-use/function-calling/coding behavior specifically, which Zhipu emphasizes as a deliberate training-data and evaluation priority for this generation rather than a side effect of general instruction tuning. The exact RL algorithm, reward design (verifiable-reward-style as in R1, versus a learned reward model, versus some hybrid), and the specific agentic-trajectory data pipeline are not disclosed by Zhipu at a technical-report level comparable to DeepSeek-R1's paper — treat the *existence* of an RL-based reasoning-and-agentic post-training stage as confirmed by behavior and industry norms, and the *specific algorithm* as inferred rather than confirmed.

### 7. Key Research Contributions & Novel Techniques

1. **Hybrid reasoning as a single-checkpoint capability**, in the same design category as Claude 3.7 Sonnet and distinct from GPT-5's multi-model-plus-router system framing — GLM-4.5 is a concrete, open-weight instance of this pattern that can actually be inspected/run locally, unlike Claude's or GPT-5's closed weights.
2. **A major MoE scale-up over GLM-4's dense architecture**, reportedly reaching ~355B total / ~32B active parameters — a substantial architectural discontinuity within the same lineage's naming convention (GLM-4 dense → GLM-4.5 sparse MoE), worth calling out explicitly since the version-number continuity could otherwise mask the architecture change.
3. **Deliberate agentic/coding focus as a first-class training and evaluation priority**, rather than tool-use being an incidental capability of a generally-capable instruct model — this mirrors the industry-wide 2025 shift toward benchmarking and optimizing for agentic-harness performance (SWE-bench-style coding, tool-use suites) rather than static knowledge benchmarks alone.
4. **Fully open-weight release of a frontier-scale reasoning MoE under a permissive (MIT) license**, positioning GLM-4.5 alongside DeepSeek and Qwen as one of the strongest openly-inspectable answers to what would otherwise be a closed-model-only capability tier (hybrid reasoning at large MoE scale).

### 8. Training Challenges & Engineering Solutions

- **Expert-parallelism communication cost at ~355B total parameters** is the dominant systems challenge, structurally identical in kind (though not necessarily identical in Zhipu's specific mitigation) to the all-to-all dispatch/combine bottleneck DeepSeek's device-limited routing and communication-engineering work targets — Zhipu's specific mitigations are not disclosed at DeepSeek's level of detail.
- **Training a single checkpoint to be well-calibrated about when to think versus answer directly** is a genuine RL-training challenge shared with Claude 3.7 and any hybrid-reasoning model: the model must learn variable-length deliberation that correlates with task difficulty rather than either always padding to a fixed reasoning length or never engaging extended reasoning when it would actually help.
- **Balancing general capability against a deliberately agentic/coding-weighted training emphasis** without regressing broader instruction-following or knowledge tasks — a version of the same data-mixture-balance challenge every lab in this document set faces when upweighting a specific domain (compare Qwen2.5's math/code upsampling discussion).

### 9. Inference & Serving Considerations

GLM-4.5's serving-cost story follows the same **active-vs-total-parameter framing** already established for Mixtral (`005_Mixtral8x7B.md`) and DeepSeek-V3 (`007_DeepSeek_V3.md`) in this folder: weight storage/HBM footprint scales with the ~355B total parameter count, while per-token compute (FLOPs) scales with the ~32B active parameter count actually touched by routing for any given token — the same total/active split that makes MoE models cheaper to *run* per token than a dense model of equivalent total size, at the cost of needing enough aggregate memory (often across multiple devices/nodes) to hold every expert regardless of how many are active for a given token. **GLM-4.5-Air** exists specifically to give deployers a lighter-weight point on this same family's cost/quality curve, the same size-tiering logic behind Qwen2.5's dense ladder and DeepSeek's distilled R1 variants, just applied within an MoE family instead of a dense one. Hybrid reasoning adds an orthogonal, request-time cost lever on top of the fixed active-parameter cost: enabling thinking mode increases token count (and therefore latency and cost) proportionally to however much deliberation the model chooses to emit, exactly as it does for Claude 3.7's billable thinking tokens — a caller deciding whether to enable thinking mode is making a cost/latency-vs-quality tradeoff decision independent of, and additive to, the model's underlying MoE serving cost.

### 10. Evaluation, Benchmarks & Known Limitations

Zhipu reports GLM-4.5 as competitive with, and on specific agentic/tool-use and coding benchmarks exceeding, some Western frontier models available at release time. As with every self-reported cross-competitor comparison in this document set (Qwen2.5 vs. Llama-3.1-405B, GLM-4 vs. GPT-4, DeepSeek vs. various frontier models), these are single-lab-run numbers and should be read alongside independent evaluation rather than accepted at face value — the specific benchmarks, scaffolding, and prompting setups used to produce any "exceeds a Western frontier model" claim materially affect how much weight the claim should carry, and this document does not have independent verification of Zhipu's specific reported figures. Known limitations: training-data composition and size are not disclosed with DeepSeek/Qwen-level specificity, making it hard to attribute GLM-4.5's capability gains cleanly to data scale, architecture, or RL post-training; the precise MoE load-balancing algorithm is underdisclosed relative to DeepSeek's dedicated treatment of the same problem; and — as with every hybrid-reasoning model discussed in this collection — there is no independent guarantee that the model's visible/implied reasoning trace in thinking mode is a faithful representation of its actual internal computation, the same general CoT-faithfulness caveat raised for Claude 3.7 and DeepSeek-R1.

**GLM-4.6 coda.** Later in 2025, Zhipu released **GLM-4.6** as an iterative update rather than a new architectural generation: an expanded context window over GLM-4.5 and further agentic/coding-focused improvements, in the same spirit as a point release rather than a from-scratch retrain. It does not introduce a disclosed new mechanism beyond what GLM-4.5 established (MoE scale, hybrid reasoning, agentic focus) and is best understood as continued execution on the GLM-4.5 formula rather than a distinct model to analyze separately at the depth given to GLM-4.5 itself.

### 11. Confirmed Facts vs. Speculation

**Confirmed (from Zhipu's GLM-4.5 release materials and general public reporting):** the shift from GLM-4's dense architecture to GLM-4.5's sparse MoE architecture; the existence of a hybrid-reasoning (thinking-mode toggle) capability exposed on a single checkpoint; the reported ~355B-total/~32B-active parameter figures (flagged as self-reported, not independently re-verified); the existence of a smaller GLM-4.5-Air sibling; the MIT open-weight license; Zhipu's deliberate agentic/tool-use/coding emphasis for this generation; the Zhipu-to-Z.ai public/product branding shift around this generation; and GLM-4.6 as a later-2025 iterative update with an expanded context window and further agentic/coding improvements over GLM-4.5.

**Speculative / not fully disclosed, and explicitly flagged as such:** the exact MoE load-balancing algorithm (auxiliary-loss-style versus DeepSeek-V3-style bias-based, versus some other scheme); exact pretraining data composition, size, and any synthetic/agentic-trajectory-generation component; the specific RL algorithm and reward design used to instill hybrid-reasoning and agentic behavior; exact training infrastructure (cluster size, interconnect, expert-parallelism degree, wall-clock/cost); the precise mechanics of the thinking-mode toggle at the API level (continuous budget dial versus coarser toggle); and the independent-verification status of Zhipu's specific competitive benchmark claims against Western frontier models.

### 12. Staff/Research Interview Talking Points

- Be able to draw the three-way hybrid-reasoning contrast precisely: Claude 3.7 Sonnet (one checkpoint, continuous thinking-budget dial, visible-by-default reasoning trace) vs. GLM-4.5 (one checkpoint, a thinking-mode toggle, open-weight so directly inspectable) vs. GPT-5 (a *system* of at least two models plus a router deciding which handles a query) — same underlying trend (test-time compute as a controllable resource), three structurally different serving/product architectures.
- Be ready to place GLM-4.5's reported ~9% active-parameter sparsity ratio (32B/355B) alongside Mixtral's ~28% (12.9B/46.7B) and DeepSeek-V3's ~5.5% (37B/671B) as data points in the broader 2023→2025 trend toward larger total-parameter MoE pools at a roughly stable-to-shrinking active-parameter compute budget per token.
- Be explicit that GLM-4.5's version-number continuity with GLM-4 masks a genuine architectural discontinuity (dense → sparse MoE) — a good answer flags this rather than assuming version numbers imply architectural continuity, the same care this document applies throughout the DeepSeek V2→V3→R1 lineage.
- Know precisely which specific numbers here are self-reported-by-Zhipu-and-unverified (the 355B/32B parameter split, any comparative benchmark claim) versus which are closer to release-mechanics fact (MIT license, open-weight release, the existence of a thinking-mode toggle) — this precision-about-provenance is the throughline of every file in this collection and is itself an interview-relevant habit to demonstrate.
- Be able to connect GLM-4.5's agentic/coding emphasis to the broader 2025 industry shift toward agentic-harness benchmarking (SWE-bench-style, tool-use suites) as the evaluation frontier, rather than static multiple-choice knowledge benchmarks — and be ready to note that this shift makes cross-lab comparison *harder*, not easier, since agentic benchmark results are unusually scaffold- and harness-sensitive.
