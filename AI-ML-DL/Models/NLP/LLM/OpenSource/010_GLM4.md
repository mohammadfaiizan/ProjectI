## GLM-4 (2024)

### 1. Overview & Strategic Context

GLM-4 is the flagship 2024 generation of a lineage that traces back further than any other model in this collection so far: the **GLM (General Language Model) pretraining objective**, introduced by Zhipu AI and Tsinghua's Knowledge Engineering Group (KEG) lab in "GLM: General Language Model Pretraining with Autoregressive Blank Infilling" (Du et al., 2021; ACL 2022), scaled up in **GLM-130B** (Zeng et al., 2022, "GLM-130B: An Open Bilingual Pre-trained Model"), then carried into the open-weight **ChatGLM / ChatGLM2 / ChatGLM3** (2023) conversational models before culminating in **GLM-4** (2024). Zhipu AI is a Tsinghua-affiliated spinout (founded 2019 out of the KEG lab), which is worth knowing because it explains why the GLM research lineage reads as one continuous academic-then-commercial thread rather than a single company's isolated product line, in contrast to Meta/Mistral/Alibaba/DeepSeek's more product-first framing elsewhere in this folder.

Strategically, GLM's lineage matters for two separable reasons, and it is important not to conflate them. First, **the GLM objective itself is a genuine, citable research contribution**: a single pretraining task (autoregressive blank infilling) explicitly designed to subsume BERT-style bidirectional understanding and GPT-style autoregressive generation, rather than requiring a lab to choose an encoder, a decoder, or an encoder-decoder architecture and accept that architecture's characteristic weaknesses on the other family's tasks. Second, **ChatGLM-6B (March 2023) was historically significant as a distribution event**: it was one of the first strong, openly downloadable, bilingual (Chinese/English) chat models broadly usable outside the Western-lab-dominated open-model landscape, arriving in the same early-2023 wave as Llama's initial (unauthorized-leak-then-official) spread, and materially widened who could self-host a credible conversational LLM. GLM-4 (2024) is Zhipu's attempt to compete at the GPT-4 tier: the largest GLM-4 variant is kept proprietary/closed-weight (accessed via Zhipu's API), while **GLM-4-9B** is released open-weight, alongside multimodal **GLM-4V** variants. As with every lab's cross-competitor benchmark claims discussed elsewhere in this document set, Zhipu's own comparisons of GLM-4 against GPT-4-class models should be read with the same skepticism applied to any single lab grading its own homework; the architectural and objective-level facts below (the GLM objective, its 2D positional scheme, GLM-130B's disclosed scale) are, by contrast, well-documented in peer-reviewed and arXiv papers and are treated here as confirmed.

### 2. Architecture Deep Dive

**The GLM objective: autoregressive blank infilling.** This is the one genuinely distinctive research idea underpinning this entire lineage, and it is worth deriving mechanically rather than just naming it.

Given an input token sequence x = [x₁, ..., xₙ], GLM samples several non-overlapping spans of contiguous tokens to remove (span lengths drawn from a Poisson distribution with λ=3 in the original paper, with the total masked span length constrained to roughly 15% of the sequence — deliberately matching BERT's 15% masking rate so the two objectives are comparable in "how much of the input is hidden"). Two masking regimes are used depending on generation: **[MASK]** for several short scattered spans (favoring an understanding/infilling behavior), and **[gMASK]** for a single long span running from a random cut point to the end of the sequence (favoring long-form generation behavior, since predicting "everything after this point" is structurally equivalent to open-ended continuation). GLM-130B's pretraining mixes both regimes, with generation-oriented [gMASK] given more weight, plus a small (~5%) multi-task instruction-pretraining (MIP) component folded in to improve zero-shot behavior — the paper discloses this mixture at a summary level; exact per-regime token ratios are not treated here as precisely nailed-down beyond "gMASK-weighted."

The corrupted sequence is split into two parts, and this split is the key mechanical idea:

- **Part A**: the original sequence with each sampled span collapsed to a single mask placeholder token, retained in its original left-to-right order. Every token in Part A can attend to every other token in Part A **bidirectionally** — non-causally, exactly like a BERT encoder.
- **Part B**: the content of the masked spans, with the spans **randomly permuted** (not necessarily left-to-right relative to their original positions) and concatenated. Each span's content is generated **autoregressively**: token i of a span is predicted from Part A (all of it, bidirectionally) plus every Part-B token that precedes it in the permuted generation order — exactly like a GPT decoder, but conditioned on a bidirectionally-encoded context rather than a purely causal one.

The resulting attention pattern is a **prefix-LM mask**: full bidirectional self-attention within Part A, causal self-attention within Part B, and Part B tokens may attend to all of Part A while Part A tokens may never attend to Part B. One shared transformer, one attention-mask pattern, one cross-entropy loss (computed only over Part B's predicted tokens) does the work that would otherwise require BERT's separate masked-language-modeling pretraining plus GPT's separate causal-LM pretraining.

**Why this differs from BERT.** BERT predicts each masked position independently in a single non-autoregressive forward pass — it has no mechanism for token j of a masked span to condition on token j-1 of the *same* span, because both are predicted simultaneously and independently. GLM instead generates an entire masked span token-by-token, autoregressively, so within-span dependencies (e.g., producing a coherent multi-word phrase, not just independently-plausible single tokens) are modeled directly.

**Why this differs from GPT.** GPT applies strict left-to-right causal masking to the *entire* sequence, including whatever should function as "context" — there is no non-causal attention anywhere. GLM instead grants full bidirectional attention specifically to the surviving (unmasked) context, and reserves causal masking only for the spans being actively generated, which is what lets one model serve understanding-style tasks (where seeing the whole input matters) and generation-style tasks (where autoregressive continuation is the point) from a single pretrained checkpoint.

**2D positional encoding — the mechanism that makes span permutation work.** Because Part B's spans are randomly reordered relative to their original positions, a single 1D position index would not tell the model *where in the original sequence* a given Part-B token's content belongs, nor *how far into its own span* it is. GLM instead assigns every token a **pair** of position ids: **position-id-1** is the token's position in the original, uncorrupted sequence (for a Part-B token, this is the position of the [MASK]/[gMASK] placeholder it is filling — every token generated for a given span shares the same position-id-1, since they all fill the same original slot); **position-id-2** is the token's offset within the span currently being generated (0 for every Part-A token, since they are not part of any generation; 1, 2, 3, ... for successive tokens within a Part-B span). Each id is mapped through its own embedding table and the two embeddings are summed into the token's positional representation. This lets the model recover both "which original slot does this belong to" and "how far along the generation of that slot am I," decoupled from the token's literal sequential position in the corrupted/reordered input — which is precisely what a naive single position index cannot express once spans are permuted.

**GLM-130B-specific engineering** (disclosed in the GLM-130B paper, distinct from the GLM objective itself, which predates it): DeepNorm (a post-layer-norm variant chosen for training stability at 70 transformer layers), GeGLU-gated FFN activations, and rotary position embeddings layered on top of the 2D scheme above. **An important, honestly-flagged nuance for GLM-4 specifically**: by the ChatGLM2/ChatGLM3/GLM-4 generations, practical usage converged heavily toward the [gMASK]-style single-trailing-span regime — i.e., ordinary causal, left-to-right generation conditioned on a prefix — which behaviorally looks much more like standard GPT-style autoregressive decoding than the original multi-span-with-permutation regime this section derives. Zhipu's public GLM-4 materials do not give a crisp, paper-level statement of exactly how much of the original multi-span blank-infilling machinery is retained in GLM-4's own pretraining versus how much the recipe has simplified toward conventional causal LM pretraining with occasional infilling objectives mixed in; this document treats the *original GLM objective's mechanics* (above) as confirmed history and GLM-4's *exact* pretraining-objective mixture as one of the genuinely underdisclosed points (see Section 11).

### 3. Scale — Parameters, Data, Compute

- **GLM-130B** (2022): 130B dense parameters, bilingual (English/Chinese), trained on a reported **~400B tokens**. Training used a reported cluster of **96 DGX-A100 (40GB) servers (768 A100 GPUs)** over roughly two months, per Zhipu's own disclosures.
- **ChatGLM-6B** (March 2023): 6B parameters, bilingual, reported training corpus on the order of ~1T tokens, 2K native context, with INT4-quantized inference reported to fit within ~6GB of GPU memory — a deliberately consumer-hardware-friendly release.
- **ChatGLM2-6B** (June 2023): same 6B scale, context extended to 32K, reported training corpus increased to ~1.4T tokens, adds Multi-Query Attention (MQA) for inference efficiency (see Section 9).
- **ChatGLM3-6B** (October 2023): same scale class, adds native tool-calling/code-interpreter/agent data to the SFT stage; incremental over ChatGLM2 rather than a new pretraining run at a new scale.
- **GLM-4** (2024): exact parameter count for the flagship closed variant is **not disclosed** by Zhipu. **GLM-4-9B** is released open-weight at 9B dense parameters, with a base/chat split and reported context variants up to 128K, plus a longer-context **GLM-4-9B-Chat-1M** variant reportedly supporting up to 1M tokens of context. **GLM-4V-9B** is the corresponding open-weight multimodal (vision-language) variant.
- As with Qwen2.5 and DeepSeek elsewhere in this folder, treat the smaller open-weight releases (ChatGLM-6B/GLM-4-9B) and the larger closed flagship (GLM-130B is the exception — it *was* released, GLM-4's largest tier is not) as separate disclosure tiers: what is known in detail scales inversely with model size across this lineage.

### 4. Training Infrastructure & Distributed Training

GLM-130B's paper is the most infrastructure-forthcoming document in this lineage: training combined data, tensor, and pipeline parallelism (standard 3D parallelism) across the 96-node A100 cluster, using mixed-precision (FP16) training. A specific, disclosed engineering response to loss spikes at this scale was an **embedding gradient shrink** strategy (dampening gradient magnitude flowing into the embedding layer, which the paper identifies as a disproportionate source of training instability at 130B scale) combined with DeepNorm's improved gradient stability at depth. ChatGLM2 onward discloses comparatively little about training infrastructure beyond noting the use of standard efficient-attention kernels (FlashAttention) for longer-context training. GLM-4's training infrastructure (GPU count, interconnect, parallelism strategy, wall-clock/cost) is **not publicly disclosed** at anywhere near GLM-130B's or DeepSeek-V3's level of detail — this is a genuine disclosure gap in the lineage, not an oversight in this document.

### 5. Pretraining Data & Objective

GLM-130B: the mixed [MASK]/[gMASK] autoregressive blank-infilling objective described in Section 2, applied over a bilingual (English/Chinese) corpus of roughly 400B tokens, plus a small multi-task instruction-pretraining component. ChatGLM/2/3: SFT-stage bilingual dialogue data layered on top of a GLM-pretrained base, with ChatGLM3 adding structured tool-use/function-calling/code-execution examples to the instruction data. GLM-4: Zhipu's own retrospective paper ("ChatGLM: A Family of Large Language Models and Multimodal Models from GLM-130B to GLM-4," 2024) describes continued scaling of pretraining data and a broadly similar family lineage, but — consistent with the Section 2 caveat — does **not** disclose GLM-4's exact pretraining token count, data composition, or the precise blank-infilling-vs-conventional-causal-LM mixture at anything like GLM-130B's level of specificity. Treat GLM-4's pretraining-data scale and objective mixture as one of this file's flagged uncertain points rather than a confirmed fact.

### 6. Post-Training / Alignment Approach

ChatGLM/ChatGLM2/ChatGLM3 follow a conventional SFT (+ some RLHF-style preference alignment) pipeline layered on the GLM-pretrained base, with each successive ChatGLM release adding more/better instruction and (from ChatGLM3) agentic tool-use data rather than introducing a new alignment algorithm. Notably, the same Tsinghua KEG lineage also produced **P-Tuning v2**, a parameter-efficient prompt-tuning technique widely used by the open-source community to cheaply fine-tune ChatGLM-family checkpoints on custom data without full-parameter fine-tuning — a genuinely influential adjacent contribution from the same research group, distinct from the GLM pretraining objective itself. GLM-4 is aligned via SFT plus RLHF-style human-feedback training per Zhipu's disclosures, with an explicit emphasis on function-calling/tool-use behavior in the instruct-tuned checkpoints, foreshadowing the much heavier agentic emphasis of GLM-4.5 (covered in the next file in this collection).

### 7. Key Research Contributions & Novel Techniques

1. **The GLM objective itself** — autoregressive blank infilling with permuted-span generation and 2D positional encoding, unifying bidirectional-understanding and autoregressive-generation pretraining into a single task rather than requiring separate encoder-only/decoder-only pretraining recipes. This is the headline contribution of the entire lineage and predates GLM-4 by two years.
2. **INT4 quantization of a 100B+-parameter model without significant quality loss**, demonstrated in the GLM-130B paper, enabling inference on a single server of consumer-class GPUs (e.g., RTX 3090s) rather than requiring multi-node A100/H100 infrastructure — a meaningful democratization-of-access contribution for its era (late 2022, predating the Llama-driven open-model wave).
3. **P-Tuning v2**, a parameter-efficient fine-tuning technique from the same lab, widely adopted across the ChatGLM open-source ecosystem.
4. **ChatGLM-6B as an early, credible bilingual open chat model**, historically significant for broadening access to strong conversational LLMs outside the initially Western-lab-dominated open-model landscape.
5. **GLM-4-9B / GLM-4V-9B's long-context and multimodal open-weight releases**, extending the lineage's open tier into the 128K/1M-context and vision-language space.

### 8. Training Challenges & Engineering Solutions

- **Loss spikes at 130B scale under FP16 mixed precision**: addressed via the embedding-gradient-shrink strategy and DeepNorm's improved training stability at depth, both explicitly disclosed in the GLM-130B paper.
- **Serving a single objective well on both understanding and generation benchmarks**: achieving this in practice (rather than just in objective design) required the disclosed [MASK]/[gMASK] curriculum mixture, weighted toward the generation-oriented [gMASK] regime specifically because generation ability was judged harder to obtain incidentally than understanding ability.
- **Balancing bilingual (Chinese/English) data representation** across the corpus, a recurring challenge for every non-English-centric lab in this document set (compare Qwen2.5's and DeepSeek's analogous multilingual-balance disclosures).
- **Quality-preserving post-hoc INT4 quantization** at 130B scale, requiring careful calibration to avoid the accuracy collapse naive low-bit quantization typically produces at that parameter count.

### 9. Inference & Serving Considerations

GLM-130B's INT4 quantization story is the standout serving contribution: a 130B dense model runnable on a single server of consumer GPUs, in sharp contrast to the multi-A100/H100-node requirement of comparably-sized contemporaries. ChatGLM2 introduces **Multi-Query Attention (MQA)** specifically for inference efficiency — MQA is the extreme end of the GQA spectrum discussed throughout this folder (n_kv_heads = 1, i.e., every query head shares a single KV head, versus GQA's n_kv_heads = some small group count > 1), giving the smallest possible KV cache footprint at the cost of the most aggressive K/V sharing; Zhipu reports this contributes materially to ChatGLM2's faster inference and longer feasible context relative to ChatGLM-6B's plain multi-head attention. GLM-4-9B's serving profile is otherwise the conventional dense-model story (compute and cache scale with the disclosed 9B parameter count and whatever attention-head configuration Zhipu ships), and its 1M-context variant raises the same long-context KV-cache-cost concerns discussed for DeepSeek-V2's and Qwen2.5's post-hoc context extensions elsewhere in this folder.

### 10. Evaluation, Benchmarks & Known Limitations

GLM-130B's paper reports performance matching or exceeding GPT-3 (175B) on a range of English and Chinese benchmarks at roughly two-thirds the parameter count — a headline efficiency claim from the paper itself. GLM-4's benchmark positioning against GPT-4-class models is **Zhipu's own self-reported comparison** and should be read with the same skepticism applied to any lab's cross-competitor claims elsewhere in this document set; independent, non-Zhipu-run replications of GLM-4's headline numbers are considerably thinner in the Western ML community than the equivalent independent scrutiny Llama, Mistral, or even DeepSeek have received, partly because GLM-4's largest variant is not open-weight and therefore cannot be independently probed at that scale. Known limitations: the flagship GLM-4 tier's exact architecture and scale are undisclosed, limiting independent verification; GLM-4V's multimodal benchmark claims are similarly self-reported; and the lineage's Western-ML-community mindshare and third-party benchmarking coverage remain smaller than Llama/Mistral/DeepSeek/Qwen despite GLM-130B predating most of them.

### 11. Confirmed Facts vs. Speculation

**Confirmed (from the original GLM paper, the GLM-130B paper, and Zhipu's ChatGLM/GLM-4 release materials):** the autoregressive blank-infilling objective's mechanics (Part A/Part B split, prefix-LM attention pattern, span permutation, 2D positional encoding with position-id-1/position-id-2), GLM-130B's 130B parameter count, bilingual English/Chinese training, ~400B training tokens, 96×DGX-A100 training cluster, DeepNorm/GeGLU architectural choices, embedding-gradient-shrink stability trick, INT4 quantization results, ChatGLM/ChatGLM2/ChatGLM3's release cadence and headline features (6B scale, MQA introduction in ChatGLM2, 32K context, tool-use data in ChatGLM3), and GLM-4-9B/GLM-4V-9B's open-weight release with 128K/1M context variants.

**Speculative / not fully disclosed:** GLM-4's exact pretraining objective mixture (how much, if any, of the original multi-span blank-infilling regime survives versus a simplified [gMASK]-dominant/causal-LM-like recipe — flagged explicitly in Section 2 and Section 5 as genuinely uncertain rather than glossed over); GLM-4's largest closed-variant parameter count, training data composition and size, and training infrastructure; and the independent-verification status of Zhipu's GPT-4-class comparative benchmark claims for GLM-4.

### 12. Staff/Research Interview Talking Points

- Be able to derive the GLM blank-infilling objective from first principles on demand: the Part A/Part B split, the prefix-LM attention pattern this implies, and precisely why 2D positional encoding is necessary once spans are permuted (a single position index cannot simultaneously express "which original slot" and "how far into this span's generation"). This is the mechanism most likely to separate genuine understanding from having merely heard the phrase "blank infilling."
- Be able to state precisely how GLM's objective differs from BERT's (span-level autoregressive dependency modeling vs. independent per-position prediction) and from GPT's (bidirectional attention over surviving context vs. pure left-to-right causal masking over everything) — this is the cleanest three-way contrast available in this entire document collection.
- Know the historical timeline cold: GLM (2021 paper) and GLM-130B (2022) predate ChatGPT's November 2022 launch and the 2023 Llama-driven open-model wave; ChatGLM-6B (March 2023) was an early, credible, non-Western bilingual open chat model in that same wave, not a Llama-derivative.
- Be ready to place MQA (ChatGLM2), GQA (used throughout this folder, e.g., Qwen2.5/Mixtral), and MLA (DeepSeek-V2/V3/R1) on a single spectrum of KV-cache-vs-quality tradeoffs: MQA is the most aggressive literal K/V sharing (n_kv_heads=1), GQA is a tunable middle ground, and MLA replaces literal sharing with a learned low-rank latent reconstructed at attention time — a strong connective-tissue answer given all three appear in this collection.
- Be explicit, unprompted, about what is and isn't disclosed for GLM-4 specifically (Section 11) — precision about the boundary of public knowledge is itself the staff-level signal, not confidently reciting a number that isn't actually published.
