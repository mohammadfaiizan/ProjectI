## PaLM 2 (2023)

### 1. Overview & Strategic Context

PaLM 2 (Google, May 2023 -- announced at Google I/O, described in the "PaLM 2 Technical Report") is the direct successor to PaLM, and it is strategically notable for an unusual reason: Google explicitly marketed it as a **smaller** model than PaLM that **outperforms** PaLM on the large majority of benchmarks, including reasoning, coding, and (especially) multilingual tasks. This is the single most important framing fact about PaLM 2 for an interview context -- it is the clearest real-world, frontier-scale case study of the shift from Kaplan-style scaling intuition (bigger is better, parameters are the lever) to Chinchilla-style compute-optimal scaling (for a fixed compute budget, there is a joint optimum over parameters *and* tokens, and most pre-2022 large models, including the original GPT-3/PaLM generation, were arguably over-parameterized relative to the data they were trained on).

The PaLM 2 technical report is deliberately less detailed than the PaLM paper on exactly the dimensions that would let a reader reproduce or precisely benchmark the model -- no official parameter count, no fully specified architecture diagram, no complete training-token count. This is a meaningful shift in disclosure posture: PaLM was published as a research paper with full transparency; PaLM 2 is published as a technical report in the same genre later occupied by the Gemini family, i.e. partial disclosure calibrated to competitive concerns as much as scientific transparency. Treat every specific number in this file as coming from leaks, third-party estimates, or careful reading of hedged report language unless stated otherwise.

### 2. Architecture Deep Dive

Google's own disclosure is thin here by design. The technical report states that PaLM 2 uses "a new architecture" and "diverse mixture of pre-training objectives," but stops short of giving the layer count, attention variant, or activation function in the way the PaLM paper did. What is explicitly stated:

- **Compute-optimal scaling** informed the choice of both model size and token count, explicitly citing the compute-optimal scaling law literature (i.e., the Hoffmann et al. 2022 "Chinchilla" result) as the guiding methodology, rather than simply scaling up PaLM's recipe.
- **A UL2-style mixture of pretraining objectives.** UL2 (Tay et al., 2022, also a Google paper) trains on a mixture of denoising objectives spanning span-corruption (T5/BERT-style masked-span infilling), prefix-LM, and standard causal LM, unified under a single framework with "mode tokens" that tell the model which objective is in effect. PaLM 2's report references improving the model's training objective along these lines rather than using pure next-token prediction throughout, though it does not give the exact mixture ratios.
- **Substantially improved multilingual and reasoning/coding data composition** relative to PaLM's corpus, described qualitatively as "more diverse" without a precise breakdown.

What is *not* disclosed: parameter count, exact layer/head/dimension configuration, exact attention mechanism (whether MQA/GQA carries over from PaLM is not confirmed), and precise token count. Treat any architecture diagram purporting to show PaLM 2's exact internals as unofficial reconstruction, not confirmed fact.

### 3. Scale -- Parameters, Data, Compute

This is the section where PaLM 2's disclosure posture matters most. Google has never officially published a parameter count for PaLM 2. Contemporary reporting (most prominently a widely circulated 2023 report citing internal Google documents, via the technology press) suggested a figure in roughly the 300-billion-parameter range -- notably smaller than PaLM's 540B -- but this number was never confirmed by Google and should be treated as an industry estimate, not a fact on par with PaLM's disclosed 540B. The technical report itself only states, qualitatively, that PaLM 2 is smaller than the largest PaLM model while achieving better results on the majority of evaluated benchmarks, which is the load-bearing claim regardless of the exact figure.

The report also discloses that PaLM 2 was trained on a data volume "significantly larger" than PaLM's 780B tokens, and that this reflects a deliberate compute-optimal re-balancing: given a comparable or larger total training compute budget, more of that budget was allocated to *tokens seen* and comparatively less to *parameters added*, following the Chinchilla-style finding that most models of PaLM's era were too large for the amount of data they were trained on (equivalently, undertrained relative to their parameter count). Google also reports four size tiers used across products, internally codenamed Gecko (smallest, edge/on-device-capable), Otter, Bison, and Unicorn (largest), corresponding to different points on the latency/cost/quality frontier -- but no parameter counts are attached to these tiers either.

**The core scaling-law point to be crisp about in an interview:** Kaplan et al. (2020), the scaling-law paper behind GPT-3-era sizing decisions, recommended allocating additional compute mostly toward parameters, with comparatively modest token-count growth, because of how they fit their loss-vs-compute curves. Hoffmann et al. (2022, "Chinchilla") re-derived the compute-optimal frontier more carefully (correcting for learning-rate schedule confounds in the original fits) and found the actual optimal allocation is roughly equal in the *exponents* on parameters and tokens -- i.e., for a fixed compute budget, most contemporary large models (including GPT-3 and, implicitly, PaLM) were substantially over-parameterized and under-trained relative to the compute they consumed. Chinchilla itself (70B parameters, ~1.4T tokens) demonstrated this by *outperforming* the much larger Gopher (280B parameters, ~300B tokens) at similar total training compute. PaLM 2 is the direct large-scale sequel to that demonstration inside a single lab's own model lineage: same organization, explicit statement that they applied compute-optimal scaling, smaller resulting model, better resulting quality.

### 4. Training Infrastructure & Distributed Training

The PaLM 2 technical report gives essentially no infrastructure detail comparable to PaLM's Pathways discussion -- there is no equivalent "MFU" figure, no chip-count disclosure, and no description of pod topology. It is reasonable to infer continuity with the Pathways system and TPU v4 (and by this point, TPU v5e was becoming available) given Google's stated infrastructure trajectory and the fact that PaLM 2 shipped from the same organization roughly a year after PaLM validated Pathways at scale, but this is inference, not something the report states. Do not present specific chip counts or pod configurations for PaLM 2 as confirmed; there are none to cite.

### 5. Pretraining Data & Objective

The report describes a training corpus that is markedly more multilingual than PaLM's -- explicitly built to substantially improve non-English performance, including on translation, and evaluated on a wider span of languages than the original PaLM benchmark suite. It also emphasizes a larger proportion of code and reasoning-relevant data relative to PaLM's mixture, cited as the driver behind PaLM 2's reported improvements on coding and multi-step reasoning benchmarks. As noted in Section 2, the objective is a mixture of pretraining tasks in the UL2 tradition rather than pure causal LM, though exact objective-mixture weighting is not disclosed. No exact token-count figure, source breakdown percentages, or filtering methodology is published at the level of detail PaLM's paper provided for its own 780B-token mixture.

### 6. Post-Training / Alignment Approach

The technical report discusses PaLM 2's use as the base for instruction-tuned and RLHF'd downstream products (it explicitly underlies the-then Bard chatbot and various API-facing "text-bison"/"chat-bison" style endpoints across the Gecko/Otter/Bison/Unicorn tiers), and mentions standard responsible-AI evaluation and red-teaming processes consistent with Google's broader model-release practice, but does not give a detailed methodological account of the RLHF/instruction-tuning pipeline comparable to, say, InstructGPT's paper. Treat the alignment methodology as "consistent with contemporary industry RLHF + SFT practice, not independently detailed by Google for this model" rather than assuming any specific disclosed technique.

### 7. Key Research Contributions & Novel Techniques

The headline contribution is not a new architectural primitive; it is the **empirical demonstration, at frontier industrial scale and from a single lab's own model lineage, that a smaller model trained compute-optimally beats a larger model trained the old way.** This matters for a staff-level discussion because it moved the compute-optimal scaling argument from an academic result (Chinchilla, a comparison between two models from the same paper, both smaller than frontier scale at the time) to a shipped-product-level decision at the very top of the market -- Google chose to *ship* a smaller model as the upgrade, and framed that choice publicly as the correct application of compute-optimal scaling, which is a strong signal about how seriously frontier labs internalized the Chinchilla correction.

Secondary contributions: the UL2-style multi-objective pretraining mixture as a way to get benefits associated with span-corruption/infilling objectives (useful for tasks like code infilling) without abandoning the causal-LM backbone needed for open-ended generation; and a much stronger multilingual result than PaLM, attributed to deliberate corpus rebalancing rather than any architectural change.

### 8. Training Challenges & Engineering Solutions

Because the technical report withholds most systems-level detail, this section is necessarily thinner than PaLM's. What can be said with confidence: achieving "smaller and better" is not merely a data-ratio change decided in isolation -- it requires the pretraining data pipeline, filtering, and multilingual sourcing to be substantially reworked (more multilingual, more code, more reasoning-oriented content) to make the additional token budget actually useful, since compute-optimal scaling only pays off if the additional tokens are high-quality and non-redundant; simply repeating the same corpus for more steps would hit diminishing returns well short of Chinchilla's predicted gains. Google's own framing supports this: the report attributes gains not to token count alone but to token count *combined with* an improved, more diverse dataset mixture and improved objective -- i.e., the engineering challenge was as much a data-curation problem as a scaling-law arithmetic problem.

### 9. Inference & Serving Considerations

A smaller model with better quality is a direct serving-cost win, and this is very likely part of why Google was comfortable disclosing the "smaller" framing publicly even without giving an exact parameter count: it is a strategically favorable claim to make regardless of the precise number, since it implies lower inference cost per query at equal-or-better quality relative to PaLM. The four size tiers (Gecko/Otter/Bison/Unicorn) additionally suggest a deliberate, disclosed-in-spirit-if-not-in-number product strategy of offering multiple points on the cost/latency/quality frontier from a single model family, with Gecko explicitly described as small enough for on-device/edge deployment -- a serving consideration entirely absent from the PaLM release, which shipped only as large research-scale checkpoints.

### 10. Evaluation, Benchmarks & Known Limitations

PaLM 2 is reported to outperform PaLM on the large majority of the evaluated benchmark suite, with the most pronounced gains on multilingual tasks, reasoning benchmarks, and coding tasks. It also reportedly closed much of the gap with (and on some tasks matched or exceeded) contemporary competitor models at the time of release. Known limitations: because parameter count, exact data mixture, and infrastructure are not disclosed, PaLM 2 cannot be independently reproduced or precisely audited the way PaLM can; and because it was rapidly followed within roughly six months by Gemini 1.0, PaLM 2's window as Google's flagship model was short, and public technical interest in it was quickly superseded by interest in Gemini's native multimodality story.

### 11. Confirmed Facts vs. Speculation

**Confirmed (stated in the technical report):** PaLM 2 is smaller than PaLM's largest (540B) configuration; it was trained using compute-optimal scaling methodology informed by the Chinchilla line of work; it uses an improved, more multilingual and code/reasoning-heavy data mixture; it uses a mixture of pretraining objectives in the UL2 tradition; it outperforms PaLM on most evaluated benchmarks; four internal size tiers (Gecko, Otter, Bison, Unicorn) existed across products.

**Speculation / unconfirmed:** the exact parameter count (widely reported estimates, commonly cited around the ~300B range, but never confirmed by Google -- treat any specific figure as an industry estimate); the exact token count; the exact architecture (layer count, head count, attention variant, activation function); exact training infrastructure (chip type/count, pod topology); whether MQA/RoPE/SwiGLU/parallel-block choices carried over unchanged from PaLM (plausible given organizational continuity, but not stated).

### 12. Staff/Research Interview Talking Points

- Be able to state the Kaplan-vs-Chinchilla distinction precisely: Kaplan et al. (2020) recommended growing parameters faster than tokens under a fixed compute budget (partly due to a confound with learning-rate schedules in their fits); Hoffmann et al. (2022) corrected this and found near-equal scaling of parameters and tokens is compute-optimal, demonstrated concretely by Chinchilla (70B) beating Gopher (280B) at similar compute.
- Use PaLM-to-PaLM2 as the frontier-industry case study of that correction being applied at a scale far beyond Chinchilla's original academic comparison, and be ready to explain *why* this required more than just "use fewer parameters" -- it required sourcing enough additional high-quality, diverse (especially multilingual and code) tokens to make the added data budget actually useful rather than redundant.
- Be explicit, if asked for PaLM 2's parameter count, that Google never confirmed one, and give the caveat on any number you cite (e.g., "reporting at the time suggested roughly 300B, unconfirmed by Google") rather than stating it as fact -- this distinction (confirmed vs. leaked/estimated) is itself a signal of calibrated technical communication that interviewers at this level are checking for.
- Be ready to connect this to serving economics: a smaller-and-better model is a strictly dominant move for a company serving billions of queries, which is a good angle for discussing why compute-optimal scaling matters as much for production cost as for research elegance.
- Note the shift in disclosure norms between PaLM (full research paper) and PaLM 2 (technical report, partial disclosure) as an early instance of the pattern that becomes the norm for the entire Gemini family -- useful context for calibrating expectations going into the Gemini files.
