## Claude 2 (2023)

### 1. Overview & Strategic Context

Claude 2 shipped in July 2023, roughly four months after Claude 1, with a follow-up point release (Claude 2.1) in November 2023 that pushed the context window further and reduced hallucination rates. The two headline, publicly stated improvements over Claude 1 were: (1) a jump in context window from Claude 1's 9,000 tokens to **100,000 tokens** (Claude 2.1 later doubled this to 200,000), and (2) measurable gains on coding and quantitative-reasoning benchmarks (Anthropic cited improved scores on the Codex HumanEval coding benchmark and on the GSM8K grade-school-math benchmark relative to Claude 1). Claude 2 also opened API access more broadly and was the version made available to a wider self-serve developer audience for the first time, rather than remaining primarily partner-gated.

Strategically, the 100K context window was the standout differentiator at the time: it was, for a period in mid-to-late 2023, the largest context window offered by any widely available frontier chat model, ahead of contemporaneous GPT-4 offerings (which were generally 8K–32K at that point, before OpenAI's later 128K expansion). This let Anthropic market Claude 2 heavily around long-document use cases specifically — dropping an entire codebase, a lengthy legal contract, or a full book manuscript into the prompt and asking questions across all of it, a capability that shorter-context competitors could not match without external retrieval infrastructure. This positioning is itself a strategic bet worth naming explicitly: rather than compete primarily on head-to-head reasoning benchmarks against GPT-4, Anthropic chose long-context handling as a differentiated axis, foreshadowing the broader "capability tiering and specialization" logic that would show up again, more formally, in Claude 3's three-tier release strategy.

As with Claude 1, no architectural or scale details were disclosed for Claude 2 — the same disclosure policy discussed in `001_Claude1.md` Section 1 applies unchanged. What follows reasons carefully about the *systems and infrastructure implications* of the one hard, disclosed fact that changed materially: the context window.

### 2. Architecture Deep Dive

**Anthropic disclosed no architectural details for Claude 2** — no parameter count, no attention variant, no positional encoding scheme. Everything below is informed inference from the one concrete, disclosed number that changed: the context window going from 9K to 100K tokens (and to 200K with Claude 2.1).

**What extending context length that far implies, mechanically:**

- **Positional encoding must generalize well past training-time-typical sequence lengths, or be explicitly designed to.** A naive learned absolute-position-embedding table (one trainable vector per position index, as in original GPT-style models) has no principled way to generalize to positions it never saw during training — extending context this far with such a scheme would likely require either training on sequences at or near 100K length directly (expensive: attention cost scales quadratically in the naive dense case) or a positional scheme designed for extrapolation. By 2023, the two dominant industry approaches for this were **RoPE (rotary position embeddings)**, which encode relative position through a rotation applied to query/key vectors and have documented (if imperfect) extrapolation behavior, and **ALiBi (attention with linear biases)**, which adds a distance-proportional penalty directly to attention logits and was explicitly designed for train-short/infer-long generalization. Anthropic has not confirmed which (if either) Claude 2 uses; the inference here is only that *some* deliberate choice in this space is a near-necessity to reach 100K coherently, not a specific claim about which scheme.
- **Attention itself likely needed some efficiency treatment beyond naive dense self-attention**, at minimum at inference time. Dense self-attention is O(n²) in sequence length; at n=100,000, a naive quadratic attention pass is enormous — this is a strong, if indirect, argument that Claude 2's serving stack (whatever it is) applies at least one of: FlashAttention-style fused, memory-efficient exact-attention kernels (which reduce memory movement, not the O(n²) compute itself, but make it *practically* tractable at this length); some form of sparse or windowed attention; or grouped/multi-query attention to shrink the KV-cache specifically (see Section 9's memory analysis, which is the most concrete, quantifiable inference this file can make). None of these are confirmed for Claude 2 specifically; FlashAttention-style kernels in particular were widely adopted industry-wide by this point and are the least speculative guess among the options.
- **No disclosed multimodality** — Claude 2, like Claude 1, is text-only; vision input arrives with Claude 3 (see `003_Claude3_Family.md`).
- **Likely continuity of the decoder-only Transformer family** used across the industry and in Anthropic's own published interpretability research — there is no public signal of a departure from this family for Claude 2.

### 3. Scale — Parameters, Data, Compute

Not disclosed — Anthropic has never published a parameter count, training-token count, or FLOPs figure for Claude 2.

The informed inference available here is the same kind used for Claude 1's Instant/full split, updated with Claude 2's own data points: Claude 2 was priced noticeably below GPT-4 at the time (a widely reported comparison in contemporaneous coverage), and Anthropic continued to offer "Claude Instant" alongside full Claude 2 at a further price/latency discount. A sustained multi-tier price gap across a stable product line, where the *cheaper* tier is explicitly marketed as good for high-throughput, lower-complexity tasks and the *more expensive* tier for tasks needing stronger reasoning, is consistent with (not proof of) a real difference in either parameter count or the amount of compute spent per request between the tiers — this is the same reasoning pattern that becomes fully explicit and confirmed-by-design with Haiku/Sonnet/Opus pricing in Claude 3. Nothing here should be read as a specific number; it is a directional inference from pricing behavior only.

### 4. Training Infrastructure & Distributed Training

Not disclosed for Claude 2 specifically. The generic requirements of training any frontier-capability model in this period apply, as discussed in `001_Claude1.md` Section 4 (data/tensor/pipeline parallelism as needed by model size, optimizer-state sharding, checkpointing for fault tolerance over multi-week runs) — repeating that discussion is not useful here; the material difference worth adding for Claude 2 specifically is about what a 100K-token context implies for the *training* side, not just inference:

- **Training on long sequences is itself expensive and is usually staged.** A common, publicly documented industry pattern (used by multiple labs, though not confirmed specifically for Anthropic) is to pretrain the bulk of a model on shorter sequences (cheaper, since attention cost scales quadratically with sequence length) and then run a comparatively short **long-context extension/fine-tuning phase** at or near the target context length near the end of training, sometimes combined with position-encoding adjustments (e.g., RoPE base-frequency rescaling, sometimes called "position interpolation," was a documented open-research technique circulating in this period for exactly this purpose). Whether Anthropic used a staged approach like this for Claude 2 is not confirmed, but it is the standard, lower-cost way labs in this period achieved long-context models without training every step at the full target length, and is offered here as a plausible, generic technique rather than a Claude-2-specific fact.
- **Data for long-context training needs actual long-range structure to be useful.** Training a model to *use* a 100K window well requires training examples where information genuinely spans that range (e.g., long documents, concatenated related documents, long code repositories) — simply padding short documents to a long nominal length would teach positional mechanics but not the actual skill of retrieving and integrating distant information, a distinction Anthropic's own product marketing (emphasizing "give it a whole book / whole codebase") implies they cared about, though the specific data curation approach used is not disclosed.

### 5. Pretraining Data & Objective

Not disclosed in composition, size, or cutoff methodology. As with Claude 1, the safe generic inference is a standard causal next-token prediction objective over a large, predominantly web-derived corpus, plausibly with code and long-form document sources weighted specifically to support the long-context capability being marketed (see Section 4). Claude 2's publicly stated knowledge cutoff was early 2023, an advance of roughly a year and a half over Claude 1's, consistent with a genuinely newer pretraining data snapshot rather than only a fine-tuning-stage change.

### 6. Post-Training / Alignment Approach

Claude 2's alignment approach is understood to be a continuation of the same Constitutional AI-based pipeline introduced for Claude 1 (SL-CAI self-critique-and-revision, followed by RL-CAI reinforcement learning against an AI-feedback-trained preference model — see `001_Claude1.md` Section 6 for the full mechanical description, which is not repeated here) rather than a publicly documented new alignment technique. Anthropic's public materials around Claude 2 emphasized continued work on reducing harmful outputs and, notably, **reducing hallucination rates** as an explicit stated improvement area — Anthropic's own release messaging claimed Claude 2 (and especially the follow-up Claude 2.1) produced measurably fewer unsupported/incorrect factual claims in internal evaluations, framed as progress on the "honest" leg of the HHH triad specifically.

One documented, Claude-2.1-specific nuance worth noting for anyone reasoning about the honesty axis: independent and Anthropic-reported evaluation of very long context usage surfaced a "needle in a haystack" retrieval concern — a long-context model can *have* the relevant fact within its 100K window and still fail to surface or use it correctly, which is a distinct failure mode from either hallucination (fabricating an answer) or a knowledge-cutoff gap (not having seen the fact at all during pretraining). Anthropic's public commentary around the Claude 2.1 update specifically addressed prompting techniques (e.g., instructing the model to first quote relevant sentences from the context before answering) as a partial mitigation, which is itself informative about how brittle long-context retrieval could still be even at a stated 200K window, independent of alignment training per se.

### 7. Key Research Contributions & Novel Techniques

- No new alignment technique was publicly introduced with Claude 2 specifically beyond continued application and refinement of Constitutional AI from Claude 1 — the primary technical news was the context-length jump and the associated engineering (undisclosed in mechanism) required to make a 100K/200K window usable in practice rather than nominally supported but ineffective.
- Anthropic's public commentary on long-context reliability (the "needle in a haystack" prompting guidance for Claude 2.1, and general acknowledgment that raw context length and effective use of that context are different properties) is itself a small but genuine research/product contribution: it publicly named a distinction — nominal context window versus effective long-context recall — that became a broader industry evaluation concern (and a standard "needle in a haystack" benchmark methodology used across labs afterward) rather than something Anthropic alone continued to grapple with quietly.
- Continued, incremental improvement on coding (HumanEval) and math (GSM8K) benchmarks was cited by Anthropic as a headline result, though without a mechanistic explanation of what training changes drove it (more code data, more RL fine-tuning on reasoning traces, or scale increases are all plausible and undisclosed).

### 8. Training Challenges & Engineering Solutions

Not disclosed for Claude 2 specifically. The generically-applicable, non-Claude-2-specific engineering challenges of extending effective context length that far are worth naming here, since they are the one dimension where Claude 2 made a large, quantified leap:

- **Quadratic attention cost.** Naive self-attention compute and (without mitigation) memory both scale as O(n²) in sequence length; going from 9K to 100K is roughly a 123x increase in the attention matrix's element count per layer per head, which is the central reason long-context serving requires either algorithmic mitigation (efficient attention kernels, sparsity) or simply a large amount of additional compute/memory budget, or both.
- **KV-cache memory growth at inference time**, which is large enough to deserve its own quantitative treatment — see Section 9 and the accompanying Python file, which implements a KV-cache memory calculator specifically to make this concrete rather than asserting it qualitatively.
- **Position-encoding generalization**, discussed in Section 2 — whatever scheme is used has to remain well-behaved (not producing degenerate attention patterns) across a range of positions two orders of magnitude larger than the prior model's supported range.
- **Evaluating long-context quality is itself nontrivial** — standard perplexity or short-context QA benchmarks don't exercise the "find a needle in a haystack of unrelated distractor text spanning tens of thousands of tokens" skill at all, meaning a lab pushing context length has to build or adopt new evaluation methodology specifically for it (as Anthropic and others did, converging on variants of the "needle in a haystack" test), not just report the old benchmark suite at a bigger window size.

### 9. Inference & Serving Considerations

This is the section where a 100K context window has the most concrete, quantifiable implications, independent of any undisclosed architecture detail — and it is the focus of this file's accompanying Python implementation (`002_Claude2.py`), which computes exactly this.

**The core scaling relationship.** For a standard multi-head-attention Transformer decoder generating autoregressively, the KV-cache — the stored key and value vectors for every previous token, at every layer, needed so each new token's attention doesn't recompute keys/values for the whole prefix from scratch — has memory footprint:

```
KV-cache bytes = 2 (K and V) x num_layers x seq_len x num_kv_heads x head_dim x bytes_per_element x batch_size
```

The factor of 2 is because both keys and values are cached. Three properties of this formula matter for reasoning about Claude 2 specifically, even without knowing its real layer count or hidden size:

- **It is linear in sequence length**, unlike the O(n²) compute cost of the attention operation itself — so a 100K-token KV-cache is (all else equal) roughly 11x larger than a 9K-token one, not 123x. Memory pressure from long context is a real but *more tractable* problem than the raw attention-compute scaling would naively suggest, which is one reason serving very long context windows is feasible at all without requiring a proportionally enormous compute increase per request, at least on the memory-footprint axis specifically.
- **`num_kv_heads` is the single largest available lever for reducing this cost**, independent of everything else in the model. If a model uses standard multi-head attention (num_kv_heads == num_query_heads), the KV-cache is as large as the formula above computed at full head count. If instead it uses **multi-query attention (MQA)** (`num_kv_heads == 1`, all query heads share one K/V head) or **grouped-query attention (GQA)** (`num_kv_heads` some small divisor of the query head count, e.g., 8 query heads sharing 1 or 2 KV heads), the KV-cache shrinks proportionally — this was an actively-discussed industry technique by 2023 specifically *because* labs were pushing context length and needed to control serving memory. Anthropic has not disclosed whether Claude 2 uses MQA/GQA or standard MHA; it is flagged here as the most consequential undisclosed lever specifically because it is the one that most directly determines whether serving a 100K-context request is memory-cheap or memory-expensive.
- **Batching and long-context requests trade off directly.** Because KV-cache memory scales linearly with both sequence length and batch size, a server provisioned to batch many concurrent short-context requests per accelerator has to serve many fewer concurrent long-context requests on the same hardware — a 100K-token request can, depending on model size and head configuration, consume as much KV-cache memory by itself as dozens or hundreds of short-context requests combined. This is a first-order capacity-planning constraint for any provider serving a 100K+ context model as a shared API, not a Claude-2-specific detail, but Claude 2 is the point at which Anthropic had to have solved it operationally to ship the feature at all.

The Python file's `kv_cache_report()` function walks through this arithmetic concretely across a range of plausible (labeled as illustrative, not confirmed) model-size and head-configuration assumptions, showing the qualitative conclusion above in actual gigabyte figures rather than just asserting it.

### 10. Evaluation, Benchmarks & Known Limitations

Anthropic's own release comparisons for Claude 2 emphasized improvement over Claude 1 on: **Codex HumanEval** (code generation, pass@1), reported by Anthropic to have risen substantially versus Claude 1; **GSM8K** (grade-school math word problems), also reported improved; and a broad multi-subject knowledge benchmark comparable in spirit to MMLU. Relative to GPT-4 (the strongest publicly available model at the time), Claude 2 was generally understood, including in Anthropic's own comparative framing, to trail on many raw-reasoning benchmarks while being competitive or ahead specifically on long-document tasks by virtue of context length alone.

**Known limitations as understood at the time:** despite the stated improvement, hallucination was not eliminated, only reduced; the "needle in a haystack" effective-long-context-recall gap discussed in Section 6 meant the 100K/200K window was not uniformly reliable across its full length, particularly for information placed in certain positions within the context (a pattern later more rigorously characterized across the industry, sometimes summarized as a "lost in the middle" effect, where information near the very start or very end of a long context tends to be recalled more reliably than information buried in the middle); and Claude 2 remained text-only, with no vision or other modality support.

### 11. Confirmed Facts vs. Speculation

**Confirmed (publicly stated by Anthropic or directly observable):**
- Released July 2023; Claude 2.1 follow-up in November 2023.
- Context window: 100,000 tokens at launch, extended to 200,000 tokens with Claude 2.1.
- Anthropic-reported improvements over Claude 1 on Codex HumanEval (coding) and GSM8K (math) benchmarks.
- Anthropic-reported reduction in hallucination rate for Claude 2.1 relative to earlier versions, and published prompting guidance (quote-relevant-text-first) addressing long-context retrieval reliability.

- Broader, more self-serve API availability than Claude 1.
- Knowledge cutoff advanced to roughly early 2023.

- Whether Claude 2 uses standard multi-head attention, multi-query attention, or grouped-query attention for its KV-cache.

- Whether long-context capability was achieved via staged short-then-long training or trained at target length throughout.
- Any pretraining data composition, token count, or FLOPs figure.
- Any specific attention-kernel implementation (e.g., FlashAttention) used in training or serving.

### 12. Staff/Research Interview Talking Points

- Be able to derive the KV-cache memory formula from first principles (2 x layers x seq_len x kv_heads x head_dim x bytes x batch) and explain why it is linear in sequence length even though attention compute is quadratic — these are genuinely different scaling curves and conflating them is a common interview-level mistake.
- Be able to name GQA/MQA as the standard lever for controlling KV-cache growth at long context lengths, and explain the mechanism (fewer distinct K/V projections shared across more query heads) without needing to claim Anthropic actually uses it for Claude 2 — the goal is fluency with the technique family, not a false claim about Claude 2's internals.
- Be able to articulate why "100K context window" and "reliably uses all 100K tokens" are different claims, and connect this to the "needle in a haystack" / "lost in the middle" evaluation literature as the field's response to that gap.
- Be able to reason (without overclaiming) about why long-context training is typically staged (short-sequence pretraining, then a shorter long-context extension phase) rather than trained at full target length from step one, purely from the O(n²) attention-cost argument.
